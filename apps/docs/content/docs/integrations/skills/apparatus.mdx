Apparatus builds an interactive explainer for a React Native or Expo app. Ask
for one in your own words — "explain how rate limiting works", "show how a
spring animation settles" — and the agent designs a small machine whose working
is the idea, writes it as a component, and checks it running.

## What it makes

One figure, as a component in your project:

- **A machine** drawn in isometric with react-native-svg, from parts that belong
  to it — rails, knobs, coils, gates, a cut-open casing.
- **A model** in plain TypeScript. The motion, the readout and the caption all
  come from it, so the drawing cannot claim something the numbers do not.
- **Touch** that runs the machine: drag a carriage, flick a wheel, tap a lever.
  The figure only claims the touches it needs, so the screen still scrolls.
- **A frame** built from PanelUI's [Frame](/docs/components/frame) parts: title,
  figure number, a live readout and a one-line hint, with a caption underneath.
- **Accessibility**: a role, a value read from the model, and actions for
  everything a finger can do.

Motion runs on the UI thread with Reanimated. Each moving part is its own native
layer, transformed rather than re-rendered, so React renders the figure once.

## Requirements

The figure needs react-native-svg 15, Reanimated 3 or 4, Gesture Handler and
PanelUI, either the `panelui-native` package or components copied in with the
CLI. `expo-haptics` is optional: with it, knobs click through their detents.

The skill copies its drawing kit into your project, about five files, the first
time it builds a figure. Later figures reuse it.

## Asking for one

Name the idea and, if you like, what the reader should be able to do. The skill
picks the machine.

- "Explain how a debounced search waits for the user to stop typing."
- "Show how a list view reuses its rows when it scrolls."
- "Make an onboarding figure for how end-to-end encryption works."
- "Explain how a car engine works."

A figure takes most of an hour to build properly. The agent says how large the
figure will be before starting, and asks first if it is a big one.

## The examples

Five figures built with the skill ship in its `examples/` folder, where the agent
reads them as references. They are also in the example app's Skills section.

### Spring bench

A carriage on a rail, held by a coil spring and an oil dashpot. Drag it out and
let go: the spring is stiffness, the dashpot is damping, and a pen on the
carriage draws the motion onto paper feeding down. Two knobs change the spring
and the damping.

### Flywheel

Momentum scrolling. Flick the wheel and it keeps your speed, then the brake
takes the same share of it every millisecond. The strip across the top is the
list's content. Touch the wheel to stop it; the knob sets the deceleration rate.

### Token hopper

Rate limiting with a token bucket. Each tap is a request. While there is a ball
in the magazine, the request goes through: the ball drops through the gate and
rolls down to the tray. When the magazine is empty the request is refused and a
flag goes up. The feeder adds one ball every 0.8 s.

### Four-stroke engine

An inline four cut open down its cylinders. Each piston runs intake,
compression, power and exhaust over two turns of the crankshaft, and the four
fire in the order 1, 3, 4, 2. Drag sideways to turn the crank by hand; the knob
on the throttle sets the speed.

A cell on a slide, cut in half. Tap to feed it glucose. The molecule comes in
through a transporter and splits as it crosses the cytoplasm, which nets 2 ATP.
The halves then go into a mitochondrion, which releases about 28 more.

## What it does

1. **Sizes the figure** — a sketch, a figure or a showpiece — and asks before a
   large one.
2. **Writes the model** with the real numbers: rates, ratios, counts.
3. **Invents the machine** and lists its parts.
4. **Builds the geometry** once per screen size, and splits the drawing into
   layers wherever a part moves between two others.
5. **Makes it live**: one frame loop on the UI thread, gestures, haptics, and a
   demonstration when nobody is touching it.
6. **Verifies it running** on a simulator or a device, recording the motion and
   reading it frame by frame.

## Limits

It is for explaining how something works. For data, use the
[charts](/docs/charts/bar-chart); for decoration, a still illustration is
cheaper.

Figures are drawn in four greys from your theme and one accent, your primary
colour. They follow light and dark mode and every theme, but they are not
full-colour illustrations.

The skill targets React Native and Expo. It does not build figures for the web.
